# Disclaimer

**This is not legal advice, and it is not a lawyer.**

`creator-contract-redline` is a checklist and a set of editing tools. It helps an
AI assistant read a contract, compare it against a fixed list of things that are
commonly one-sided in creator agreements, and write suggested changes into the
document as tracked changes. That is drafting assistance. It is not a legal
opinion about your contract, your situation, or your rights.

## No attorney-client relationship

Using this skill does not create an attorney-client relationship with anyone —
not with the author, not with Anthropic, not with whoever ran it for you.
Nothing produced by it is privileged or confidential in the way that
communications with your own lawyer are.

## It will be wrong sometimes

The output is generated by a language model. It can misread a clause, miss one
entirely, misstate what a term means, cite a section that does not exist, or
produce a suggested edit that says something different from what it claims to
say. The skill includes gates specifically because dropped and mis-applied edits
are its characteristic failure — those gates reduce that risk, they do not
eliminate it.

**Read the redline before you send it.** Read the cover note before you send it.
Read the contract before you sign it. A suggested edit you have not read is your
edit once you send it, and it is your signature on the agreement.

## It does not know your law

Contract law varies by jurisdiction and by subject matter. Enforceability,
penalty clauses, unconscionability, employment classification, consumer
protection, advertising regulation and disclosure obligations all differ
depending on where you are and what you are doing. This skill applies a general
checklist. It does not research your jurisdiction, and it does not know facts
about your business that are not in the documents you gave it.

## Commercial judgment is yours

Which asks to make, which to trade away, what your rates are, and whether to
sign at all are business decisions. The skill is built to hand those decisions
back to you rather than make them, but you are the one making them.

## Get a lawyer for anything that matters

On a deal of meaningful size, or where an agreement carries uncapped liability,
a personal guarantee, an assignment of rights, exclusivity that affects your
other work, or anything you would find painful to be wrong about, have a
qualified attorney in your jurisdiction review the final document. The cost of
an hour of review is almost always smaller than the cost of the clause you
missed.
